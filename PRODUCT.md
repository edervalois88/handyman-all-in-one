# PRODUCT.md — HandyMan All-in-One

## What this is

HandyMan All-in-One is a **residential home repair and maintenance company**. It is not a
marketplace, not a lead-gen directory, and not a gig platform. The customer hires HandyMan —
never an individual worker. HandyMan schedules the job, quotes the price, dispatches the crew
(or a licensed trade partner when the scope legally requires one), and owns the result.

The product being sold is **accountability**: one company, one standard, one point of contact
for the entire home.

## Unique mechanism (one sentence)

HandyMan is the only home-services company where a single accountable business — not a
rotating cast of independent contractors — answers the phone, quotes the price before work
begins, sends qualified in-house technicians plus licensed trade partners when required, and
stands behind the finished job.

## Audience and their real scene

**Primary: the homeowner who is tired of chasing people.** Roughly 35–65, owns a single-family
home, works full-time, has a list of six to twenty small-to-mid jobs that have been accumulating
for months. Not necessarily handy, not interested in becoming a project manager.

Their actual scene: it's a Tuesday evening. There's a slow drip under the sink, a bedroom door
that won't latch, a screen with a hole in it, and a hallway that needs paint. They have already
texted two contractors this month. One never replied. One replied, scheduled for Thursday, and
never showed. They are not looking for the cheapest bid — they are looking for someone who
**answers, arrives, and stands behind it**. The dominant emotion is not price sensitivity, it
is **exhaustion with unreliability**.

**Secondary:** property managers and small landlords with recurring turnover work; adult
children booking work on behalf of aging parents; new homeowners who just closed and have no
contractor relationships yet.

**Their alternative today:** calling a different specialist per trade, using an app-based
marketplace that sends a stranger, or doing nothing and living with the list.

## What the visitor must believe, and what earns it

| Belief to install | What earns it |
|---|---|
| Someone will actually answer | A real phone number, visible hours, a form that confirms |
| Someone will actually arrive | A named scheduling window, job status updates, on-time promise |
| The price won't move | "We quote first" — approved pricing before work begins, no hidden costs |
| One company owns the whole list | Services grouped across trades under one company, not a per-trade referral |
| The work is safe and legitimate | In-house technicians **plus licensed trade partners when the law requires it** |

Every one of those is a **claim, not a demonstrated fact** for a new business. The site must
therefore present them as commitments in the brand's own words, and must **not** invent
customer counts, review scores, years in business, license numbers, awards, or star ratings.

## Brand truth (locked — from the brand book, not negotiable)

- **Brand essence:** "Your home, handled." — One company. One standard. One point of contact.
- **Positioning:** "The trusted team for every home project." Local-handyman convenience with
  professional-company reliability, organization, and accountability.
- **Mission:** Make home repair simple through reliable service, clear pricing, and quality work
  done by people we would trust in our own homes.
- **Vision:** Become America's most trusted all-in-one home-services company.
- **Promise (four beats, exact):** WE ANSWER · WE ARRIVE · WE QUOTE FIRST · WE STAND BEHIND IT.
  - We answer → clear and responsive communication
  - We arrive → reliable scheduling and updates
  - We quote first → approved pricing before work begins
  - We stand behind it → consistent quality and dependable support
- **Values (exact):** RELIABILITY (we do what we say) · TRANSPARENCY (no hidden costs or
  surprises) · CARE (we respect every home as if it were our own) · RESPONSIBILITY (we know when
  a licensed specialist is required)
- **The problem, in the brand's words:** homeowners are tired of calling multiple contractors,
  waiting for no-shows, and managing different people for every small repair. The problem is not
  finding someone who can do the work — it is finding someone reliable enough to answer, arrive,
  and stand behind it.
- **Solution, in the brand's words:** HandyMan manages small-to-mid-sized home projects through
  qualified in-house technicians and licensed trade partners when required.
- **Voice:** plain, calm, adult, specific. Blue-collar professional. Short declarative sentences.
  No hype, no exclamation marks, no "best in town", no exclamation-driven urgency, no emoji,
  no "we're passionate about". Talks like a competent tradesperson who keeps their word.
- **Mark:** walking worker silhouette carrying a ladder, holding a paint bucket with a brush —
  vintage workwear illustration. Wordmark "HANDYMAN" over "ALL - IN - ONE" over the tagline
  "YOUR HOME, HANDLED".
- **Palette:** Midnight Navy `#1D2945` (primary; backgrounds, vehicles, uniforms, type) ·
  Warm Cream `#F4EDDA` (primary neutral; backgrounds, reversed logos) · Workwear Red `#C74732`
  (primary accent; CTAs, signage, small graphic details) · Utility Gold `#D69A3A` (secondary
  accent, used sparingly) · Soft Sage `#8A9A83` (supporting, maintenance/outdoor).
- **Type:** the book names Acumin Pro Condensed (title/subtitle/text) and Lato as the alternate.
  Neither is freely licensable for web. Substitutes must preserve the *condensed, heavy,
  workwear-signage* character of the wordmark.
- **Art direction:** the book's own mood board is 1950s–60s American suburbana — chrome car
  radios, payphones, a Sony Walkman on a green ground, a navy/cream checkerboard, a vintage TV,
  a boy on a Schwinn, a drive-in sign, wood-paneled dashboards, a jukebox. It is warm, nostalgic,
  mid-century, and physical. This is the cultural home, and it is **not** the category's default
  of stock photos of smiling technicians in polo shirts.

## The page this category always ships (the rut — keep it out)

Navy hero, stock photo of a smiling man in a polo holding a drill, "Your Trusted Local Handyman",
a row of four generic icon cards (Plumbing / Electrical / Carpentry / Painting), a five-star
review carousel, three checkbox "why choose us" bullets, a phone number, a quote form. Its
predictable opposite — the "bold disruptor" version — is black, one neon accent, and oversized
grotesk type. Neither is the build.

## Surface scope

**Mode: Persuade.** A complete, production-ready marketing site, bilingual (English default,
Spanish toggle), US market, US phone and address formats.

Pages/routes:

1. **Home** — the full narrative: hero, the reliability problem, the four-part promise, services,
   how it works, why HandyMan (vs. the alternatives), service areas, what we don't do / when we
   bring a licensed partner, FAQ, and a strong closing CTA.
2. **Services** — the full service catalog grouped by category, each with what's covered and
   typical scope, plus handyman-vs-licensed-trade boundaries.
3. **Service areas** — the coverage story (placeholder metros, clearly marked for replacement).
4. **About** — brand story, mission, values, the standard we hold.
5. **Contact / Request a quote** — the conversion form with job details.

Global: sticky header with click-to-call, language toggle, footer with hours and coverage.

## Truth and placeholder list (must ship clearly marked)

Uninventable — ship as obvious, single-source placeholders the owner replaces:

- Phone number, email, physical address, service-area city list, business hours
- License numbers, insurance limits, years in business, warranty term
- Prices, trip fees, minimum job size, response-time guarantees
- Real project photography, real customer reviews, real team photos

Authorable at full fidelity (label synthetic where a visitor could mistake it for real):

- Illustrative service icons drawn in the brand's own vintage-workwear grammar
- Diagrams of the scheduling/quoting flow
- Editorial layout, typography, and motion

## Success

A homeowner lands, understands in one viewport that this is one company that answers, arrives,
quotes first, and stands behind the work, and can start a quote or tap to call without scrolling
back. Bilingual parity is real, not a stub. Deployed live on Vercel from a public repo.

## Standing commitments

- Bilingual EN/ES is a brand commitment, not a feature toggle.
- The brand-book palette is law; Workwear Red is reserved for actions and small graphic details,
  never for large backgrounds (the book says so explicitly).
- No fabricated social proof. Ever.
