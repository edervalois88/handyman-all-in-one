## The rut, named and excluded

The category always ships: navy hero + stock photo of a smiling technician in a polo + "Your
Trusted Local Handyman" + four generic icon cards + an invented five-star carousel + three
checkmark bullets + a phone number. Its predictable opposite is black, one neon accent,
oversized grotesk. **Neither is the build.** The brand book's own 1950s–60s suburbana mood board
is the cultural home, and it is pinned by the brief.

## The build: The Work Order

The one artifact that makes this brand's claim *checkable* is the piece of paper the customer
actually signs. "We quote first" is not a slogan — it is a line on a work order with a price and
a signature and a date stamp. So the site is built as the paperwork of a well-run service
company: cream carbon-copy stock, navy ink, dot-matrix and typewriter registers, rubber stamps
in Workwear Red, tabbed index cards, perforated tear edges, and a job ticket that moves through
named stages the visitor can read at a glance.

**Donor raise — from the exposure record (declined): tonal discipline.** That world's real
strength is that meaning rides on a finite, numbered tonal ramp instead of on arbitrary color.
Raised into this build: the palette is closed and enumerated — cream and navy carry the page,
Workwear Red appears *only* as stamp/state/action, Utility Gold only as highlighting, Soft Sage
only as the outdoor/maintenance register. No improvised tints, no decorative color.

**Donor raise — from the drum machine (competitive): the sixteen-step row and the chase light.**
That world's real strength is that a temporal plan becomes spatially legible and "where now" is
always shown. Raised into this build: the scheduling-window picker and the job-status timeline,
where the visitor sees the sequence and the current stage rather than reading prose about it.

**Donor raise — from Versailles (declined): verifiable state.** That world's strength is that
every element carries a conservation state the visitor can inspect. Raised into this build: every
service line states plainly whether HandyMan does it in-house or brings a licensed trade partner,
because that boundary is exactly what the brand's RESPONSIBILITY value claims.

**Donor raise — from the papercut (declined): depth with no shading.** That world's strength is
that layers build depth purely by overlap. Raised into this build: stacked paper — sheets, tabs,
and stamps overlapping with real elevation and zero gradients, so the page has dimension without
a single soft blend.

## Honest risk

Administrative-paperwork aesthetics can read cold, and a homeowner's decision here is emotional
(exhaustion with being let down, relief at being handled). Mitigation carried in the build: the
raised paper stays warm and physical rather than clinical, the brand's worker mark keeps a human
figure on the page, and the copy stays in the brand's plain spoken voice rather than form-legalese.
